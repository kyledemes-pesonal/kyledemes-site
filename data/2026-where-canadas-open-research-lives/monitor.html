Canadian Open Access Monitor

Moniteur canadien du libre accès

How much Canadian research is free to read, how it got there, and how ready tri-agency-funded papers are for the 2026 Tri-Agency Open Access Policy, which applies to grants awarded from 1 January 2027. Built on open data from OpenAlex. Think of it as the starting line, not a compliance report: papers published so far fall under the 2015 rules.

The agencies' baselines and ours

The agencies published two KPIs on 5 October 2026 and will track them "using open data sources"; I couldn't find their method. Ours, from OpenAlex, run higher: about 6 points for Canada and 5 to 10 for funded papers. For funded papers the likely reason is how funding is detected: for closed papers it comes mostly from what publishers deposit with Crossref, while OpenAlex can also find funders in full text, which it mostly has for open papers. Both count papers that are free to read anywhere, which is not the same as meeting the new policy.

How the papers became open

The Canadian repository line falls after 2019. Some of that is lag, since deposits often come after publication. Some is harvesting that is still being fixed (see Repositories below). Some may be authors no longer depositing papers that are already open through read-and-publish deals, which would be a finding in its own right. Which of these dominates is being checked.

Repositories: how they reach this monitor

The repository numbers are only as good as the harvesting behind them, and Canada has made that harder than it needs to be. France runs one national repository, HAL, that every institution deposits into and every librarian can help curate. Canada took a hybrid route: many universities run the same software through Scholaris, the shared service from Scholars Portal, but each has its own instance and its own harvest address, which changes when a repository moves.

In September, OpenAlex audited the 26 live Scholaris repositories. Only 2 were being harvested, because most had migrated and we were still reading their old addresses. We repointed and registered all of them, and today all 26 are harvested, about 595,000 records. In a test set of 392 known deposits, the number we could find went from 195 to 242. A few repositories outside Scholaris still show sudden drops in recent years, and those are being chased now.

We're committed to keep working with the Scholaris community so OpenAlex indexes as much of Canada's repository content as it can. Refinements, like a view by university, will follow as the harvesting does.

Method, caveats and known issues

  • Source and date. The OpenAlex API, queried on the date at the top of the page. Each edition will be pinned to a dated snapshot so a change in the data never looks like a policy effect.
  • Counting rules. Articles and reviews only. A paper is Canadian if any author has a Canadian affiliation, and counted once nationally. A paper is tri-agency if its funding metadata names CIHR, NSERC, SSHRC (or their programs), Canada Research Chairs, CERC or CFREF.
  • Funder detection. Closed papers are less often tagged with a tri-agency grant than open ones (16% against 28% in 2025). That isn't a detection artifact: Crossref funder data reaches OpenAlex for closed papers too (none missed in a sample of 119), and open papers are tagged at the same rate with or without full text. Funded papers have gone open faster. Papers whose publisher deposits no funder data at all remain invisible.
  • Free to read is measured today, not at publication. It includes papers made open by an open licence on the publisher's site (for example, hybrid articles on ScienceDirect under a CC licence) and papers with a repository copy. OpenAlex keeps improving how it detects open access, so figures can shift slightly between editions.
  • Routes. Gold: OA journal with an APC. Diamond: OA journal with no APC known, which in OpenAlex includes some journals whose APC is simply missing. Hybrid: openly licensed in a subscription journal. Green: repository copy only. Bronze: free on the publisher's site, no licence.
  • Diamond in funded research. Diamond is 9% of all Canadian research but about 4% of tri-agency papers. Part of that is real: Canadian diamond publishing is concentrated in the humanities and social sciences and in clinical, professional, government and student journals, where grants are less common. Part is measurement: only 13% of Canadian diamond papers list any funder in Crossref, against 55-59% of gold and hybrid papers, so funded diamond papers are undercounted, especially for SSHRC.
  • Canadian repository copy means a record in any of the 100 largest Canadian institutional repositories and platforms. It is what OpenAlex can see: harvest gaps undercount it, and records without files overcount it. Copies on Scholars Portal Journals, which the policy also accepts, are not yet counted. An independent study found a mean 4.2% of U15 papers in the authors' own repositories (Riddle et al., 2023).
  • APC value is the journal's list price for each tri-agency gold or hybrid paper, counted whole even when co-authors abroad paid. It is not what Canada paid: read-and-publish agreements cover much of it. Butler, Matthias, Simard, Mongeon and Haustein (2022) used the same approach for 2015-18 and found US$27.6M at the five largest publishers; on that scope OpenAlex finds a near-identical set of papers (11,180 against their 10,989).
