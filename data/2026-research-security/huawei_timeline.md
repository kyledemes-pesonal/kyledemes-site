# Huawei and Canadian research: how governments and universities responded

Built 18 Nov 2026 as context for the OpenAlex analysis of Huawei in Canadian research papers. Each row cites a page that was opened (fetched and read), not a search snippet. Where only a snippet or a secondary summary was available, the row says so and is flagged **UNCONFIRMED**. CBC pages (cbc.ca) and Business in Vancouver/PressReader pages blocked automated fetching, so no CBC article is used as a source here.

General federal research-security dates (NSGRP July 2021, the 14 Feb 2023 statement, STRAC 16 Jan 2024 and 1 May 2024) are sourced in more detail in `policy_timeline.md` in this folder. This file adds the Huawei-specific layer.

## Bottom line

- **No federal rule names Huawei for research.** The 19 May 2022 decision bars Huawei and ZTE from telecom networks and says nothing about universities. The 14 Feb 2023 and 16 Jan 2024 statements name no company. **Huawei is not on the Named Research Organizations (NRO) list**: the live list at science.gc.ca was checked 18 Nov 2026 (103 entries, date modified 2024-04-18, no Huawei entity), and the copy saved 15 Nov (`../canada-parliament-2026/_factcheck/web/strac/nro_list.html`) has 0 matches for "huawei". STRAC applies to researchers affiliated with listed *research organizations*, so it does not reach a Huawei-funded partnership.
- **The real federal lever was NSERC Alliance screening under the July 2021 guidelines.** Waterloo's Huawei lab had four Alliance applications rejected as "unmitigable risks". A Liberal MP told the House science committee (SRSR) that in 2022 decisions were made "to categorically reject all applications involving Huawei". That is an MP's claim in committee, not a published NSERC rule (**flag**).
- **Federal funding of Huawei partnerships before the guidelines.** In Feb 2021 NSERC had $4.8M in Alliance partnerships that included Huawei. NSERC co-funded Huawei industrial research chairs at Polytechnique Montréal (2017) and Laval.
- **Provinces.** Alberta paused new or renewed PRC-linked partnerships on 24 May 2021 without naming Huawei. U of Calgary kept its three existing Huawei projects running into 2023–24 regardless. Ontario sent a memo, shortly after Feb 2023, saying research security applies beyond ministry-funded research; Premier Ford publicly backed Waterloo's exit on 4 May 2023. No Ontario or BC directive naming Huawei was found. Quebec ran nothing until the FRQ pilot in 2026.
- **The university exits cluster in spring 2023, about a year after the 5G ban.** Toronto decided in April 2023 (no new agreements, projects, renewals or funded extensions). McGill decided not to sign further contracts in winter 2022–23. Waterloo announced on 3–4 May 2023 that it would exit all agreements by the end of 2023. McMaster stopped approving new collaborations by May 2023. Université de Montréal said it would not renew past Dec 2024. By Nov 2023 UBC ("not undertaking any new research projects with Huawei") and Western (wind down within 16 months) had followed. Every university exit was "no new, finish existing", so Huawei-funded papers should taper through 2024–25 rather than stop. Huawei patents naming Canadian university inventors were still being published in Sept–Nov 2023.
- **Your recollection: UBC is confirmed, with a timing caveat.** UBC defended the partnership in 2019 and 2022. In May 2023 it was still "continuing to evaluate". It first said it would take no new Huawei projects in the Globe of **9 Nov 2023**, not at the time of the 5G ban. **SFU accepting Huawei grants afterwards is UNCONFIRMED.** No source found SFU taking Huawei research money after 2022. The only SFU–Huawei link found was Huawei supplying network equipment around 2017–18 for a Compute Canada renewal that SFU led, and that rests on search snippets only (BIV and Times Colonist blocked fetching). OpenAlex can test the claim directly: count SFU papers with a Huawei funder or Huawei co-author, by year.

## For the OpenAlex analysis

- Expect a gradual decline, not a cliff. The breakpoints are the July 2021 Alliance screening (new co-funded projects blocked), the May 2022 5G ban (signal only) and the spring 2023 exits (no new agreements; existing projects ran into 2024–25). Papers lag funding by 1–3 years, so Huawei-funded output from U of T, UBC, McGill, Western and Calgary should persist into 2024–26.
- Places to look for possible continuation: Queen's, Laval, Polytechnique and uOttawa (no exit statement found), and SFU (your recollection). Also Huawei-funded work routed through non-university entities such as Openmind.
- Huawei is not an NRO, so STRAC affiliation rules do not cover a Canadian author co-writing with Huawei Canada. Any drop in co-authorship after 2024 comes from university choices and NSERC screening, not from STRAC.

## Flags and gaps

1. The "categorically reject all applications involving Huawei" claim (2022) comes from one MP in committee. No NSERC or Public Safety document confirms it.
2. The Ontario memo's date and full text were not found; it is known only from Waterloo's 4 May 2023 message.
3. Alberta's May 2021 letter does not name Huawei. Calgary's Huawei projects continued after it, so it did not cover existing Huawei work in practice.
4. No BC directive was found. Quebec has no Huawei-specific measure (FRQ pilot only in 2026, see `policy_timeline.md`).
5. SFU's post-2022 Huawei funding is unconfirmed. The earlier procurement link rests on snippets only.
6. No exit statements were found for Laval, Polytechnique, uOttawa, Queen's or Alberta. Concordia, Dalhousie and York showed no Huawei partnership in what was searched. Absence of evidence is not proof.
7. CBC (Dec 2019 "$56M", Alberta May 2021, Waterloo May 2023), Business in Vancouver and PressReader blocked fetching. Their facts are used here only where another opened source repeats them.
8. The Epoch Times (Mar and May 2023) is used for university statements that match the Globe, The Logic and Academica, plus the Université de Montréal, Manitoba and Saskatchewan quotes. The U de M figure ($3.9M, Dec 2024 expiry) has a single source.
