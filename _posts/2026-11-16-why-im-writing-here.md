I've started this space to organize my thinking. Most of what I learn at work goes into emails, slide decks and calls, and then disappears. Writing it down helps me figure out what I actually think, and it gives me something to point to when people ask.

A bit about where I'm coming from. I started as a marine ecologist, studying how waves shape the evolution of kelp. I moved into research development and strategy at the Hakai Institute, UBC and SFU, helping teams build and win large research programs. Then I spent a few years at Elsevier, working with more than 40 Canadian universities and federal science departments on how they use research analytics. Today I'm COO of OpenAlex, an open index of the world's research, and I sit on the board of Canadian Science Publishing.

That path means I get to see the research system from a lot of sides at once. In a given week I might talk with a funder about tracking the outcomes of their grants, a university about its rankings, a publisher about metadata, a government about research policy, and a researcher about why their profile looks wrong. Not many roles touch all of those at the same time.

It's also a moment of fast change. Open infrastructure is replacing closed databases, AI is changing how research is found and evaluated, shifting geopolitics are reshaping who collaborates with whom, and funding is under pressure almost everywhere. Decisions being made now will shape the system for a long time.

So this is where I'll work through what I'm seeing, with data, anecdotes, and opinions. Expect posts on research funding, open science, metadata, policy, and what the numbers say about how research actually works. These views are my own, not OpenAlex's, but I'll use OpenAlex data when possible.

If something here is useful, or wrong, I'd like to hear about it. And if you'd like to hear my thoughts on a topic, shoot me an email at kyle@demes.ca.
